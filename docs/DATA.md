## Ott Deceptive Opinion Spam Corpus v1.4

- URL: https://myleott.com/op_spam_v1.4.zip (project page https://myleott.com/op-spam.html)
- SHA-256 of the zip: `e3a61b3709ccaca842aeec7f525e273988cf3b1bab35151712cf89759a6724fa`
- License: Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 3.0 Unported. The repo never
  commits the reviews. `data/op_spam_v1.4/` and `data/cache/` are gitignored.
- Fetch: `python scripts/fetch_data.py` (downloads, checks the hash, extracts to
  `data/op_spam_v1.4/`). `OP_SPAM_DIR=/path/to/op_spam_v1.4` points the loader at another copy.

Contents: 20 Chicago hotels, 20 reviews per hotel per cell. Truthful positive reviews are from
TripAdvisor; truthful negative reviews are from Expedia, Hotels.com, Orbitz, Priceline,
TripAdvisor, and Yelp; deceptive reviews were written by Mechanical Turk workers. The corpus
ships five folds, and each fold holds all reviews for 4 of the 20 hotels, so the folds are
hotel-disjoint. Four negative truthful reviews appear twice (same text, two file names, same fold).
They are kept as shipped.

Citations:

- Ott, M., Choi, Y., Cardie, C., and Hancock, J. T. (2011). Finding deceptive opinion spam by any
  stretch of the imagination. ACL-HLT. https://aclanthology.org/P11-1032/
- Ott, M., Cardie, C., and Hancock, J. T. (2013). Negative deceptive opinion spam. NAACL-HLT.
  https://aclanthology.org/N13-1053/

`data/notebook_row_order.txt` lists Ott file ids (no review text) in the row order the original
bag-of-words notebook used. It was derived once by `scripts/derive_notebook_order.py` from the
CSV export that notebook read, and it lets `src/reproduce_notebook.py` repeat that notebook's
random split exactly.

## LLM-generated reviews (`data/llm_generated/`)

400 plain-text files, `positive/positive_01.txt` to `positive_200.txt` and the same for
`negative/`. File timestamps are 2025-04-18. They were produced by `src/generator.py` and `src/main.py` (ported
here from the original working folder with the same prompt logic), with these settings:

- Model: Azure OpenAI deployment of `gpt-4o-mini`, `temperature=0.7`, `max_tokens=200`.
- System message: "You are an AI generating realistic hotel reviews."
- User prompt per review: a random persona (family with two kids, frequent business traveler,
  couple on an anniversary, budget backpacker), the requested polarity, a required opening
  clause drawn from six fixed openings ("When I arrived at the hotel", "Right after checking in",
  ...), one required seed phrase (positive: "spacious room", "river view", "helpful staff";
  negative: "dated décor", "musty smell", "weak coffee"), a target of 90 to 110 words, a mix of
  "I/we" with an occasional "they", one spatial detail, and at most two exclamation marks.
- Post-processing: output truncated to 110 words. `src/review_eval.py` scored each batch of 200
  with a TF-IDF logistic regression fit on the full Ott corpus and would regenerate the batch at
  a higher temperature if mean P(deceptive) exceeded 0.5. No hotel name is given to the model.

The files are byte-identical to the `fake_reviews_dataset/` folder (and its two zips) in the
original working directory. Nothing was edited or added after generation. The files are what the pipeline saved, which includes the `review_eval.py` check above: it could have replaced a whole batch with a regenerated one, and the logs that would say whether it did were not kept. The prompt
leaves visible traces that a detector can use, and `docs/methodology.md` describes the
"controlled" view that removes them:

| Trait | LLM reviews | Ott reviews |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starts with one of the six fixed openings | 400 of 400 | not applicable |
| Mentions a hotel name or "Chicago" | 0 of 400 | 1,260 of 1,600 (78.8%) |
| Word count | 88 to 106 (mean 98.7) | 25 to 784 (mean 148.8) |
| Contains a non-ASCII character (curly quote, é, long dash) | 208 of 400 | 0 of 1,600 |

License and terms: the reviews were generated by the repo author through their own Azure
OpenAI deployment. They are synthetic text, written to imitate hotel guests, and describe no
real stay. They are released with the repo under MIT. Label them synthetic if you reuse them.
